VDOS Correction
The original playbook runs all 8 layers simultaneously with equal weight. The VDOS data disagrees. Channel A (direct inbound via Facebook groups, WhatsApp ads, Google) scores CPI 325. Channel D (architect/developer specification) scores CPI 6,933 — a 21× difference on the same metric. Running them at equal investment misallocates capital, founder time, and sales energy.
This document preserves all 8 layers. It reorders them by CPI, assigns investment weight accordingly, adds VDOS-derived floor rates, and upgrades each message to reflect the correct conversion driver: water infrastructure independence — not aesthetics.
ASQ1 Finding
B + C = 70% of conversions. Water anxiety + property value. Rewrite all 8 layer messages from aesthetics to infrastructure independence.
ASQ4 Finding
Channel D CPI = 6,933 vs Channel A CPI = 325. Layer ordering must reflect this. Layers 1–3 receive 70% of investment. Layers 5–8 receive 30%.
ASQ6 Finding
Dormant base = R2.58M–R5.52M/yr at zero new CAC. Layer 1 (existing customers) is the highest-probability same-day revenue source. Always activated first.
Stage 1 Floor Rates
Floor rates by channel. Architect/spec: R620/m². Referral: R520/m². Contractor partner: R460/m². Direct inbound: R420/m². Non-negotiable in all 8 layers.

The Core Insight The original playbook is not wrong. It is unweighted. Every channel in it has real value. The VDOS finding is that treating them equally misallocates founder time toward the lowest-CPI channels and away from the highest. The same 100 leads, routed through CPI-weighted channels with appropriate floor rates, produce more than double the revenue — not because of better marketing, but because of better architecture.
