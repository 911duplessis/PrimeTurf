How Much Does Artificial Grass Cost Per m² in South Africa? (2026)

June 2026 · 8 min read · Written by PrimeTurf's installation team

The most common question we receive before a site consultation is some version of: "just give me a rough number per square metre." It's a reasonable question, and this guide gives you a direct answer — along with the factors that move that number up or down.

These prices reflect actual installation costs in South Africa in 2026, from PrimeTurf's direct project data across Gauteng and Cape Town.

The Short Answer: What You'll Pay Per m²

Artificial grass in South Africa is priced by installed square metre — meaning supply and labour combined, with the sub-base preparation included. Here is the current price band by grade:

Grade Pile Height Best For Installed Price per m²
Standard 25–30mm High-traffic play areas, pet runs, balconies R350 – R450
Premium 30–37mm Residential lawns, pool surrounds, entrances R500 – R650
Luxury 37–45mm Feature gardens, estates, architect specification R700 – R850
What's included in the installed price

Every PrimeTurf quote includes supply of turf, underlay, edging restraints, silica sand infill, excavation of existing lawn, sub-base compaction, laser levelling, and installation. There are no line items you discover after the fact.

What Determines Your Artificial Grass Price

1. Turf grade and pile height

The turf product itself accounts for 55–65% of total project cost. Luxury grades use denser fibre bundles, more complex blade shapes, and higher-specification UV inhibitors. For most residential applications in Gauteng, Premium grade delivers the best value-to-appearance ratio. Luxury grade is specified most often by landscape architects for estate and show-home installations.

2. Surface preparation required

This is the most variable cost element. A property with an existing natural lawn on level ground requires standard excavation and levelling — well within the base price range. But poor drainage, existing concrete, steep slopes, or tree root removal add cost. This is precisely why site consultations exist: we assess the sub-base before we quote, not after.

3. Project size

Smaller jobs (under 50m²) carry a higher per-m² cost due to fixed setup time. Projects above 200m² typically come in at the lower end of each grade's price band. When quoting, always ask for a total project price alongside the per-m² figure.

4. Location logistics

Johannesburg and Cape Town installations are similar in cost. Projects in outlying areas (Stellenbosch, Pretoria East, smaller centres) may carry a modest travel cost, which is disclosed in the quote.

Breaking Down the Cost: Supply vs Labour

Understanding what drives the price helps you evaluate quotes accurately:

  • Turf supply: typically R180–R420/m² depending on grade
  • Underlay and sub-base materials: R30–R60/m²
  • Labour (excavation, levelling, installation, finishing): R100–R180/m²
  • Edging and finishing materials: R20–R50 per linear metre of perimeter

Quotes that come in significantly below market — particularly for labour — should be scrutinised. Insufficient sub-base preparation is the primary cause of artificial grass failures within 3 years. The turf product may carry a warranty; the installation standard is what determines whether that warranty is ever needed.

Additional Costs to Budget For

Most jobs are fully covered by the per-m² quote. However, some projects require additional scope:

  • Tree stump or root removal — typically R800–R2,500 per stump depending on size
  • Drainage channel installation — required on some slopes and pool surrounds
  • Concrete breaking — if installing over failed concrete that needs partial removal
  • Irrigation capping — if an existing irrigation system runs under the lawn area

Any of these items that apply to your site will be identified at the free site consultation and included explicitly in the written quote.

The 5-year totals are comparable — and from year 6 onwards, artificial grass carries no ongoing cost while natural lawn maintenance continues. For Cape Town properties where water restriction damage adds replacement costs, the payback period shortens further.

The real value calculation

The cost model above doesn't include the intangible value: a lawn that looks the same in February as it does in June, requires nothing on a Sunday morning, and is covered by an 8-year warranty. For many homeowners, that alone resolves the question.
