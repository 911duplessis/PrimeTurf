Method & limits

This reconciliation was built from two sources only: the full WhatsApp thread "Prime Turf Marketing" (29 May – 9 Jul 2026) and the commit history of 911duplessis/PrimeTurf. No invoices, bank statements, or a written contract exist in either source — where a figure below is an estimate rather than a confirmed number, it is labelled as such. The site's first six days (29 May – 4 Jun) were built under a prior GitHub username (iamstiaan) before the account/domain migrated; those commits are visible in the history and are included, but cannot be independently cross-checked against a second source the way later work can. This is not a legal or tax document — treat it as a structured, evidence-anchored starting point for a conversation both of you need to have anyway.

Scope-boundary finding

The Connection Network platform is the single largest value item in this ledger, and it sits in a genuine grey zone. It was conceived and built during PrimeTurf's engagement window, using PrimeTurf as its first pilot — but it is designed from the outset as a general-purpose, multi-vendor SaaS product ("this doesn't have to stop at one referral... any vendor could sign up"), deployed under the developer's own AWS/Vercel accounts, not PrimeTurf's. Whether this is (a) work-for-hire PrimeTurf partly owns, or (b) the developer's own product that PrimeTurf merely piloted, was never defined in writing by either party. This ambiguity is a bigger open question than the fee dispute itself, and should be resolved explicitly in any settlement (see §7).

03 Digital strategy value — beyond the code

Separate from build hours, real strategic value exists in the record: a documented brand-positioning framework (premium/estate-lifestyle segment vs. commodity turf sellers), a 5-cluster keyword architecture with a genuine, low-competition niche identified (architect/developer specification searches), a fully wired analytics stack (GA4 + GSC + event tracking) most small businesses never set up at all, and a competitive teardown showing PrimeTurf's SEO structure already outperforming its 20-year-old market leader on indexed pages and technical hygiene.

The honest caveat

This value is currently latent, not realised. As of 9 July — six weeks in — the business has generated zero converted sales leads through any of this infrastructure. Strategic foundations (indexing, schema, GBP verification) typically take 60–90 days to show in rankings, so this is not necessarily a sign the strategy is wrong — but it means the R50,000–R150,000 estimated commercial value of this positioning work is a bet on future payoff, not cash already earned for PrimeTurf.

The three flagged scheduling incidents

Evidence supports characterising all three as normal human scheduling friction, not negligence: two of the three (11 Jun; 29–30 Jun) involved advance notice or mutual renegotiation, which is ordinary professional courtesy, not a broken commitment. The third (7 Jul) is a genuine single clerical error — misreading a stated time — with no evidence of a repeated pattern of no-shows elsewhere in 42 days of near-daily contact. What amplified its cost was timing, not severity: it landed the day before Leon's biggest accumulated complaint, and it cost a family (dealing with a recent bereavement that same week) a real two-hour wait. The underlying act was minor; the trust cost was not, because it arrived on top of an already-building deliverable-accuracy concern.

Client feedback was frequent but almost always short-form ("love it," "perfect," a thumbs-up) rather than structured sign-off. The developer asked more than once for consolidated, section-by-section feedback in a single document to avoid rework ("kry solank jou eerste paar changes reg... ons doen dit met een push"); this request was never sustained by either side. Strikingly, when Leon wrote his own process complaint on 8–9 July ("1 ding op n slag" — one thing at a time, work through it start to finish before moving on), he independently arrived at the identical fix the developer had already asked for weeks earlier. That is evidence of a shared process gap, not a one-sided failure: neither party ever installed a lightweight approval gate, and both wanted one.

Scope discipline was the weaker link on the developer's side specifically: major live changes were pushed to production more than once without a clear prior "yes" on record (e.g., 8 July, 2:18pm "if the plan is approved, say push to main," followed at 8:53pm by "49 pages have been updated" with no confirming approval message visible in between). Combined with the expanding side-projects (Connection Network, InsightForge, a Wix-vs-Claude platform debate), this is what produced Leon's "too much stuff, I can't keep track" reaction on 2 July — scope and cadence outran the client's capacity to review, and no one throttled it.

Delayed or thin feedback did cost real time: the developer was blocked or duplicating work at several points waiting for photo assets, brand-copy sign-off, and a single consolidated content document that was requested repeatedly (30 May, 6 Jun, 20 Jun) but never fully delivered in one pass. A conservative estimate is that this cost the equivalent of several days of otherwise-avoidable rework across the six weeks — a real but secondary factor next to the scope-expansion issue above.

Central finding

No invoice, day-rate, project fee, or written services agreement appears anywhere in 42 days of records. Every payment in the chat is framed by both parties as personal support — rent, food, transport, a chair, a phone — not as consideration for deliverables. The closest thing to a services agreement is Leon's 9 July offer of a 5% commission on converted leads, which is a future revenue-share, not payment for the R100k+ of work already delivered. This is the distinction the user asked this audit to draw explicitly: support-to-enable-work and payment-for-completed-work are not the same ledger, and to date only the first one has actually been running.

Read these gaps carefully rather than literally. They are not a legal debt — no rate was ever agreed, so there is no breached contract to enforce. They are a measure of opportunity cost: what the developer priced this work at zero (or near-zero) rather than what he is owed under a retroactive rate. Scenario 3's size is driven almost entirely by counting Connection Network at full standalone-product value — which, per §1's scope-boundary finding, is genuinely contestable, since the developer may be building his own asset there rather than a PrimeTurf deliverable. Scenario 1 is the most defensible floor.

07 Path forward

Option A — Continue the collaboration

  • Contract: a one-page written agreement — even informal — beats everything above it. Fixed monthly retainer (suggest R8,000–R15,000/month for continued part-time SEO/dev/marketing work) or the 5%-of-closed-leads commission already offered, not both framed as replacements for each other.
  • Separate the ledgers going forward: personal support (if it continues) should be logged as a documented loan or salary advance against the retainer — not bartered informally against "we'll sort it out later."
  • Ownership: put Connection Network in writing now — either PrimeTurf licenses it as a customer/case study with no ownership claim, or it's treated as a joint asset with defined equity. This is more urgent to resolve than the historical fee gap.
  • Cadence: one workstream at a time, written sign-off before any production push — this is the fix both parties already independently proposed and never implemented.

Option B — Conclude the engagement

  • Handover: credentials, domain, hosting, and repo access are already substantially under Leon's own logins per the chat — formalise a full access transfer checklist regardless of outcome.
  • IP: PrimeTurf keeps unrestricted use of the delivered website, SEO assets, and GBP listing already live; Connection Network's ownership question (above) gets settled explicitly rather than left ambiguous.
  • Settlement: a final lump-sum figure anchored to Scenario 1 (~R100,000 as a ceiling, negotiated down for the support already paid and the fact no rate was ever agreed) is more defensible than anything anchored to Scenario 3.

08 Final executive opinion

Neutral summary. A single, resourceful, AI-augmented developer delivered a genuinely large volume of professional-grade digital work — a full website, technical and local SEO, a competitive strategy, and a separate SaaS-grade referral platform — for a pre-revenue small business, over 42 days, in exchange for informal personal support rather than a fee. Both the scale of what was built and the informality of what was paid for it are real and well-evidenced. Neither fact cancels the other out.

Was compensation proportionate to value delivered? No, by a wide margin, under every scenario modelled. Confidence: 85%. This holds even at the most conservative valuation (Scenario 1), where roughly R8,500 was received against roughly R110,000–R120,000 of core deliverable value.

Did either party act unfairly? Low-to-moderate evidence of bad faith on either side. Confidence this is mutual miscommunication rather than exploitation: 65%. No contract ever defined "fair pay," so the underpayment is better read as a structural gap than a broken promise. Leon's frustration (0 leads after six weeks, factual overstatements on the live site, scope he couldn't track) is legitimate and proportionate to what actually happened, not an overreaction. The developer's blending of survival needs (food, transport, medication, a phone) with project requests is understandable given his stated circumstances, but it did make it harder for either side to see the commercial imbalance clearly while it was happening — that blurring, more than any single decision, is the root cause here.

What would a reasonable settlement or arrangement look like? Not a retroactive payout at Scenario 2 or 3 valuations — those numbers are useful as ceilings, not targets. The higher-value move is a written agreement covering rate, cadence, and IP ownership going forward (Option A), with a modest, negotiated acknowledgment of the historical gap (anchored near Scenario 1) if the relationship ends instead (Option B). Confidence this is the right next step regardless of which option is chosen: 80%.

One thing outside this ledger's scope

The record also documents real housing instability, food insecurity, and a request for medication tied to acute anxiety. Those are true regardless of how the commercial reconciliation above resolves, and they deserve a direct, human conversation between the two of you that a spreadsheet like this one cannot and should not try to settle.
