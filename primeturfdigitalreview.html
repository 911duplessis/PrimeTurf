03 · Method

Evidence Standard & Limitations

This report distinguishes three kinds of statement throughout:

Verified fact — confirmed directly: live site inspection, the PrimeTurf GitHub repository, sitemap/schema/robots.txt content.
Analysis — a conclusion drawn by comparing verified facts.
Recommendation — professional judgement on what to do next.

Findings also carry a confidence rating: High multiple verified sources agree · Medium limited or indirect evidence · Low inference or forward-looking.

What this version does not include

  • Semrush domain metrics (authority score, backlink count, organic traffic/keyword volume) — the connected Semrush account's plan does not include MCP data access. No figure has been estimated or invented in its place; every row that would need it is marked "not obtainable."
  • Google Search Console exports for primeturf.co.za (referenced earlier as two ZIP files) were not present in this working session, so first-party click/impression/indexing data is not cited here. Recommended as the first addition for Version 1.1.
  • Competitors' internal analytics (their real traffic, leads, conversion rate) are never visible to an outside audit — every competitor observation below is based on their public-facing site only.
Domain correction: the brief named www.easygrass.co.za as the competitor to assess. That domain does not resolve — DNS/TLS checks during this audit returned no valid response on either the bare or www host. The live, established national competitor operating in this space is easigrass.co.za (the spelling PrimeTurf's own comparison page already uses). This report assesses easigrass.co.za throughout and flags the discrepancy here rather than silently substituting it.

All figures reflect a point-in-time snapshot taken 8 July 2026. Rankings and traffic move; re-run before quoting these numbers in six months.

04 · Summary

Executive Summary

Thirty-six days ago, PrimeTurf had one indexed page. Today it has a 26-page site with working schema, 17 dedicated Gauteng location pages, a comparison page, a blog, and an automated sitemap-maintenance workflow — all verified directly against the live repository, not taken on faith. Verified fact

This isn't an existing business getting a website refresh. Registry data plus Leon's own confirmation (Section 10a) place the domain, the business, and the digital transformation at the same starting line — 1 June 2026. Everything catalogued in this report was built from zero in that window, not layered onto years of pre-existing infrastructure.

That is real, measurable infrastructure. It is also, honestly, not yet the thing Leon is asking about: closed leads. Those are two different questions, and this report is written to answer both without letting the first one drown out the second.

Analysis On technical SEO and site architecture, PrimeTurf already out-executes both named competitors — neither ArtGrass Pro nor Easigrass exposes structured data (schema) on the pages sampled, and neither builds one landing page per suburb the way PrimeTurf now does for 17 Gauteng areas. On authority — the compounding trust Google and buyers build up over months and years of backlinks, reviews, and repeat visits — PrimeTurf is exactly where a 36-day-old domain should be: near zero, competing against an 8-year-warranty Cape Town specialist and a national dealer network running 58 blog posts and a 14-region footprint.

Recommendation The right conversation is not "why no leads yet," but "is the foundation strong enough to compound." Based on what's verifiable today, yes. During this review period, a suspended Google Business Profile and a compromised Facebook account were actively capping the ceiling on any traffic this site could earn — both are now reported active (Section 02a), which removes two real constraints, though it doesn't by itself produce leads. Section 15 treats what's resolved and what's still open as separate, first-class business facts, not footnotes.

Analysis Google has repeatedly stated that domain registration age by itself is a weak-to-negligible ranking factor — what matters is consistent publishing, backlinks, and trust accumulated since a site became genuinely active, not the date a registrar record was created. That distinction matters here in a specific way: for PrimeTurf, the domain's registration date and the digital-transformation start date are effectively the same event (~37 days apart) — there is no older domain history being separated from a recent rebuild, because none exists. This section exists to give Leon an accurate register of when each domain actually began, not to claim registration age as an SEO advantage or excuse.

User-reported The business itself was founded the same day as the domain — PrimeTurf is not an existing operation that recently got a new website; company and domain both started ~1 June 2026. This is Leon's own knowledge of his business, not something a domain registry lookup can independently verify, so it carries the same evidentiary weight as the Operational Status Update in Section 02a. It means every asset catalogued in this report — the 26 pages, the schema, the 17 location pages, the blog, the Community & Authority Ecosystem — was built from literal zero in 36 days, not layered onto pre-existing digital infrastructure.

Recommendation Use these dates as a factual reference point only — e.g. "the domain is 37 days old, matching the digital transformation" — and do not describe PrimeTurf as having a long-standing domain history, since the registry record does not support that claim.

17 · Bottom Line

Conclusion

What changed in 36 days: a single unindexed page became a 26-page site with working schema, 17 local landing pages, a blog, a competitor-comparison page, and an automated sitemap workflow — all independently verified, not self-reported.

What remains unfinished: content depth (2 articles vs. a market leader's 58), any first-party Search Console evidence in this version, and confirmation that every on-site claim is currently accurate.

What's unrelated to SEO: the Google Business Profile and Facebook account — both were reported blockers during the review period and are now reported active (Section 02a), but review acquisition, sales follow-up speed, paid advertising, and brand trust remain open (Section 15).

Highest-impact next actions: maintain GBP/Facebook now that they're active, launch the Community & Authority Ecosystem strategy, move content to a monthly cadence, and re-run this audit with Search Console data once available.

What success looks like in 3–6 months: not a #1 ranking — a realistic, measurable rise in impressions and local-pack visibility for the 17 location pages, a handful of closed leads directly attributable to organic search, and a content library approaching parity with the smaller competitor (ArtGrass Pro) as a first milestone before chasing the national player.

Executive Statement

Based on the evidence reviewed — including technical implementation, website architecture, public search signals, competitor comparison, and business readiness — the work completed over the past 36 days has materially improved PrimeTurf's digital foundation.

While measurable lead growth remains constrained by factors such as domain authority, backlink acquisition, operational maturity, and the time required for organic search performance to compound, the business now possesses a significantly stronger digital asset base than at the beginning of this engagement.

This document should be treated as the baseline against which future quarterly performance reviews, marketing investment decisions, and operational improvements are measured.
